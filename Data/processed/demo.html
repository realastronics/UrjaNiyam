What did the decision change?

How the model made this happen

First, it estimated demand and the missing power. Then it searched for a lower-impact schedule under the configured rules. A separate verifier checked proposed schedules. The simulator applied the next step, updated the history and planned again.

Flexible groups have a lower interruption penalty. Normal groups have a higher one. Critical groups need explicit approval before they can be cut; approvals are declined in this demo. If a strict plan cannot be found, the fallback ladder can relax downtime limits, request approval or escalate an uncovered shortage. A time limit is not proof that no valid plan exists.

Urja Niyam is a scheduling and decision-support prototype. It does not model grid frequency, voltage, network power flow or real breaker control. A lit block means its simulated supply group is on; it does not imply measured electricity use in every building.
